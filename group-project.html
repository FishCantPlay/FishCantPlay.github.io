01 / THE BRIEF

What's the project?

A game made together

Group Project is a narrative-driven management game where the player takes the role of a student leading a university game-development team. The gameplay is built around balancing time, simplified production tasks, personal needs and relationships. Choices affect both the project and the team's morale, with failure coming from neglected work or relationships breaking down.

We wanted to make something aimed at late-teen and young-adult players, especially students and creatives. It is more about the emotional reality of trying to work together than being a complicated management simulator.

My main contributions were the full character art pipeline (concepts, models, textures, rigs and animations), all character and environmental dialogue, the 8-bit character voice sounds, and the main theme track. I have kept the team contributions separate where someone else made the work.

03 / MY PART

What I actually did

✦ Character art, models and animation

I created the character artwork and took the full cast through the 3D pipeline. This included modelling, UVs, hand-painted textures, rigging, weight painting and the character animations used in the game.

I started with simple shapes and kept the silhouettes readable, as the characters needed to work with the game's low-poly style and be recognisable from the in-game camera. I also made 2D facial animation assets for dialogue.

✦ Dialogue, voices and music

I wrote all of the character dialogue and environmental dialogue, including the smaller lines players could discover by exploring and interacting with objects. I also wrote the main dialogue and progression document, with character profiles, relationships and dialogue choices linked to the relationship system.

For the character sounds, everyone recorded their actual voice saying the vowel sounds from their character's name. I edited these recordings, added a bit-crush effect and looped them to make short 8-bit-style sounds for the characters.

I composed the main theme track. The other in-game music and additional sound effects were made by another team member, so I am not claiming those as my own work.

04 / THE PROCESS

How it developed

01. Ideas

I developed the character concepts and visual direction, using animal forms to keep the cast expressive and readable. I took inspiration from Animal Crossing, Night in the Woods and Over the Hedge.

02. Making

I moved the designs into Maya, then worked through modelling, UVs, hand-painted textures, rigging, weight painting and animation for the full cast.

03. Writing & audio

I wrote the character and environmental dialogue, made the 8-bit character voice sounds from our own recordings, and composed the main theme.

04. Final

I checked and finished the character assets for the final build, including the models, textures, rigs, animations and 2D facial assets.

05 / AUDIO & DIALOGUE

Giving the game a voice

Character voices and main theme

The character sounds were made from recordings of the actual team members. Each person recorded the vowel sounds from their character's name, and I edited these into short, bit-crushed 8-bit-style sounds. It meant the characters had their own little voices while still fitting the stylised look and feel of the game.

I also composed the main theme track in BandLab. The other game music and sound effects were created by another team member.

06 / LOOKING BACK

What I learned

This project gave me the chance to take the full character cast from 2D designs through to finished, animated 3D assets. I had to think about more than how each model looked on its own. They needed to be readable from the game's camera, fit the shared art direction and work with the rigs and animations needed in the final build.

Completing the full cast helped me become quicker and more confident with the character pipeline. There was still a difference in polish between some of the characters because of the time available, so next time I would plan the modelling, texturing and animation stages in more detail and leave more time for final checks.

Writing all of the character and environmental dialogue also made me think about how the characters' personalities and relationships come through in small interactions, not just the main conversations. Making the 8-bit voice sounds from our own recordings was a fun way to connect the team to the characters.

Overall, I developed my skills in 3D character modelling, texturing, rigging, weight painting, animation, 2D facial assets, dialogue writing and audio production. It also gave me experience of making work for a shared game, where the assets need to fit the project and actually function in-engine.
