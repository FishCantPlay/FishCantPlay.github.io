A STORY ABOUT STORIES, ESCAPISM AND CREATIVE DESTRUCTION

Bibliocide

✦ ❧ ✦

A fairytale world, a little rat companion, and a collection of stories that are not quite what they seem.

Bibliocide is a puzzle adventure game where the player travels through familiar fairytale-inspired rooms with a small rat companion, solving puzzles and recovering books before reaching the furnace at the end.

CHAPTER I

The Story

❧ ✦ ❧

Once upon a time...

Bibliocide grew out of my interest in the way stories can be used as an escape, and what happens when that escape becomes something you cannot leave behind. The player moves through a series of storybook rooms, each based on a familiar fairytale, with a small rat companion helping them solve puzzles and recover books.

The original idea I brought to the project was about a plagiarist stealing other artists' work, with burning the stolen pieces representing destruction, guilt and erasure. Once the group formed, I suggested combining that idea with fairytales. The books and final furnace kept the theme of creative destruction, while the fairytale rooms gave us a clear structure for the game.

THE WORLD OF BIBLIOCIDE Storybook rooms, fairytale references and the furnace finale
CHAPTER II

A World Between the Pages

✦ ❧ ✦
ENVIRONMENT My opening hallway, Cinderella's room, Jack's room and furnace concepts

The World

I designed concepts for the opening hallway, Cinderella's room, Jack's room and the furnace finale. I kept the opening room simple and planned for reusable assets such as boxes, furniture and candles so the visual workload stayed manageable.

PUZZLE DESIGN Room layouts, puzzle notes and rat interaction concepts

The Puzzles

The core loop was to enter a fairytale room, use the rat and environmental clues to solve puzzles, collect a book and move on to the next room. I pushed for one consistent fetch/interact behaviour for the rat rather than giving it a different ability in every level. That would make the controls easier to learn and help keep the scope under control.

CHAPTER III

Making the Story

❧ ✦ ❧

01. Research

I started with my own idea about plagiarism and creative destruction, then explored how it could be combined with fairytales and a storybook atmosphere.

02. Concept

I sketched the hallway, a sample puzzle room and the furnace to help the group understand the layout. I also explored Cinderella and the stepsisters as cardboard cut-outs, plus two different directions for the White Rabbit.

03. Production

I produced room concepts, the goose reference sheet, the rat's idle art and scratching animation, a rat run cycle, music experiments and Cinderella's recorded voice lines.

04. Evaluation

Pitch feedback supported keeping the rat's behaviour consistent and making the workload modular. Graybox testing was positive overall, although several of my finished assets had not been added to the build.

CHAPTER IV

My Part in the Story

✦ ❧ ✦

My contribution

I ended up taking on the project lead role, even though I hadn't set out to do that at the beginning. I wrote and maintained the Game Design Document, organised the direction of the project, helped shape the pitch and produced a large amount of the visual and audio work. I tried to keep the project organised and make sure everyone had a shared idea of what we were making.

My visual work included the early room and layout sketches, the Cinderella and stepsister concepts, two White Rabbit designs, the Cinderella room, Jack's room, hallway and furnace concepts, and an Embden goose reference sheet for Jane's 3D model. I also created the rat's idle UI art, its scratching animation and its run cycle in Clip Studio Paint. For audio, I made an atmospheric main menu theme in BandLab and recorded Cinderella's lines on my Blue Yeti. I also helped rewrite the Cinderella room script so clues and dialogue appeared in the right order.

MY DEVELOPMENT EVIDENCE Concept sketches, room plans, rat artwork and animation, music and voice recordings
CHAPTER V

What I Learned

❧ ✦ ❧

One of the best parts of this project was getting to work across different areas instead of only doing one type of art. I got to experiment with character concepts, environment planning, animation, music and voice acting. I was also happy that the pitch was well received and that people playing the graybox responded positively to the concept and level design.

The hardest part was balancing making the work myself with trying to keep the group organised. I spent a lot of time rewriting, clarifying and chasing things because updates were not always shared and decisions sometimes changed without everyone knowing. At the graybox test, the rat sprites, menu music, Cinderella's art and voice lines still had not been implemented, even though I had finished and submitted them earlier. That made it clear that making an asset is only one part of a production pipeline; communication and integration matter just as much. I also learned that I need to be clearer about tasks, deadlines and expectations earlier, rather than trying to fill every gap myself. My bluntness can come across harshly, so I want to get better at giving structure and feedback without letting frustration take over. Creatively, I want to keep developing how I use reference, stylisation and mood. The rat run cycle especially taught me that animation does not have to copy real movement exactly: simplifying the motion helped it fit the deliberately sketchy, unsettling storybook style.
